**Title: The Descent**  
**Chapter 1: Unnamed**  

The taxi’s air conditioning hummed like a trapped wasp, but the protagonist’s palms still slicked against the leather seat. They’d rehearsed the address three times to the driver—*“La Calle del Sol, near the old barrio”*—each syllable stiff, rehearsed, as if the words might crumble if spoken too softly. Through the window, Tucson’s Santa Catalina foothills retreated, their terraced mansions and bougainvillea-draped walls shrinking into postcard vignettes. The protagonist’s fingers traced the embossed edge of the invitation card in their pocket, its edges softened from months of nervous handling. *“Drop by anytime!”* the stranger had said at a gallery opening, laugh lines crinkling. A throwaway kindness, taken as a lifeline.  

The radio crackled to life as the driver adjusted the dial. A burst of *norteño* accordion split the silence, followed by a gravelly baritone singing in Spanish about love and revolution. The protagonist stiffened. Their own playlists were curated whispers—Chopin nocturnes, ambient nature tracks—anything to dissolve into the background. This music was *alive*, all stomping heels and defiant crescendos. They stared at the frayed hem of their slacks, wondering if asking the driver to switch stations would seem rude. The moment passed.  

Outside, the Catalina foothills gave way to the grid of midtown. Cacti and gravel yards replaced emerald lawns. The taxi descended, as if tunneling into strata of the city the protagonist had only seen in census reports: *median income*, *diversity indexes*, *property crime rates*. A mural flashed by—a painted Virgin Mary with tears like neon, arms outstretched over a carnicería. The radio’s rhythm thrummed in their molars. *What if I don’t know the customs?* they thought. *What if they expect me to dance?*  

At a stoplight, the driver glanced in the rearview. “You’re heading to a *quinceañera*?” he asked, nodding at the invitation peeking from their pocket.  

The protagonist froze. “No. Just… visiting a friend.” The word *friend* felt counterfeit. They didn’t even know the host’s last name.  

The driver grinned, gold tooth glinting. “Ah, *la vida*! Sometimes you gotta let the current take you, *mijo*.”  

The light turned green. The accordion wailed on.  

As they passed the university district, the protagonist’s phone buzzed—a reminder: *6:00 PM. Therapy Session*. Canceled, rescheduled, canceled again. Their therapist’s voice echoed: *“You’ve built a life around minimizing risk. But what’s on the other side of ‘what if’?”* The question had looped in their mind for weeks, until they’d impulsively pulled out the invitation, Googled the address, and called a cab.  

Now, the streets narrowed. Stucco houses painted mango and cobalt leaned close, their porches strung with papel picado. A trio of kids chased a deflated soccer ball, shouting in Spanglish. The radio switched to a cumbia beat, hipswaying and relentless. The protagonist’s knee bounced in time, a nervous metronome.  

When the taxi stopped, the meter read $27.50. The house before them was a coral-pink bungalow, its gate draped in fairy lights even though sunset was hours away. A dog barked. A woman laughed, loud and unselfconscious.  

The protagonist paid with exact change, fingers trembling. As they stepped onto the sidewalk, their phone rang—a number they didn’t recognize. The screen glowed: *Unknown Caller*.  

They answered, breath held.  

Somewhere inside the house, a record scratch cut the music. A voice called, “Hola! ¿Quién es?”  

The protagonist turned toward the sound, invitation clutched tight, as the voice on the phone said: *“This is St. Mary’s Hospital. Are you related to Eleanor Voss?”*  

The world tilted. Somewhere, a dog howled.
